Three hundred years ago, dragons ruled the sky over Aevum, and the ground belonged to no one. There were no clans, no borders — just people, hunted, generation after generation, until the dragons pushed hard enough that survival finally demanded one last stand. The final war was cataclysmic: new weapons were forged in it, desperate spells discovered in the worst hours of it, and when it ended, the dragons were gone — or so everyone believed. The cost was total. Entire regions were left scorched, flooded, hollowed out, and the people who'd won were left scattered across a world that no longer resembled the one they remembered.

For generations after, survival was the only concern. There was no shared purpose beyond staying alive in a broken world. Slowly — over decades, then centuries — survivors found each other again and gathered into like-minded groups. It was in that long process of regrouping, not in the war itself, that specialization emerged: smiths becoming weaponmakers, healers becoming battlefield clerics, hunters becoming those who could read terrain no one else could. What began as scattered instinct hardened, generation by generation, into the founding disciplines of the clans Aevum knows today.

Those clans now hold ground their ancestors bled for, increasingly territorial over the shrines that memorialize a war none of them lived through. Alliances form and break over old grudges dressed up as new ones. And now word is spreading: two dragons never died. They went silent instead. They've been guarding something.

The last egg of their kind is about to hatch — or it's about to be destroyed forever. Every clan in Aevum has already decided which outcome they can live with. Not all of them agree.
